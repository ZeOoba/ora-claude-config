All three were the same artifact: a monitor sampling a shared beacon mid-drain, before the producer that writes it had reached its slot in the queue. 3b already had the correct rule but had scoped it to dispatch skips, so nothing generalized it to the beacon reads in Steps 0.5 / 0.55 / 0.56 / 0.9, which is where the false findings were actually produced. One rule, applied once, in one place.

   **Ordering corollary — a monitor must not read a beacon before its producer's slot.** On a late morning the AM cluster does not preserve its designed order: cron order and drain order are different things. Before recording a beacon-derived negative, check the PRODUCER's own `lastRunAt` against today's expected slot. If the producer has not run today and its slot has passed, the reader is ahead of the writer and the negative is PROVISIONAL — full stop. See `ora-health-guard` Block B.2(C), which carries the reciprocal precondition on the am-brief Blocks B/C beacons.
4. Report the deduplicated DISPATCH-THROTTLED list in Step 1's Notion page under a new **Dispatcher-throttle** subsection and as a Step 4 summary line. Informational only — does not trigger Step 2's alert email on its own. A `taskId` appearing DISPATCH-THROTTLED on 2 consecutive runs with no fresh output either time should be escalated to `orphaned-task-forensics`. Report the PROVISIONAL count separately from the open count — a blended total is how a provisional row gets re-read as a finding on the next pass.

## Step 0.5 — Per-task expected-fire window (REWRITTEN v1.3)

For each enabled cron task, compute the most recent expected fire time `expectedFireTime` based on `cronExpression` interpreted in America/New_York. Walk backward from current time until the cron predicate is satisfied. Handle DST: EDT = UTC−4 from second Sunday of March; EST = UTC−5 from first Sunday of November. Handle DOM+month conjunctive crons (e.g., `0 9 1 1,4,7,10 *`) explicitly — v1.2 parser silently mishandled these.

For each enabled cron task, classify `lastRunAt` against `expectedFireTime` and the task's `jitterSeconds` budget:

| Classification | Rule | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **MISSED** | `lastRunAt < expectedFireTime − 30 min` | ⚠️ flag |
| **OUT-OF-WINDOW** | `lastRunAt` does NOT fall within ±60 min of ANY cron-predicate fire time in the past 7 days | ⚠️ flag (anomalous fire timing — catch-up after a missed cycle) |
| **ALERT-LATE** | `lastRunAt > expectedFireTime + 120 min` | ⚠️ flag (excessive lateness) |
| **WARN-LATE** | `expectedFireTime + 60 min < lastRunAt ≤ expectedFireTime + 120 min` | ℹ️ note (excessive lateness, no alert) |
| **VERIFIED** | `expectedFireTime − 30 min ≤ lastRunAt ≤ expectedFireTime + 60 min` | ✅ verified |

**Rationale:** v1.2's one-sided rule `Flag if lastRunAt < expectedFireTime − 30 min` caught missed fires but silently passed excessively-late fires. The 2026-05-14 deep audit revealed multiple tasks 3–5 hours late on 5/13 AND 5/14 (chronic AM dispatcher latency), and `ora-outbox-qc-sweep` firing 16.5 h outside its cron window — all silently passed by v1.2.

**OUT-OF-WINDOW detection algorithm:** generate the last 7 days of cron-predicate fire times (e.g., for `30 17 * * 1-5`: Mon-Fri 21:30 UTC); flag if `lastRunAt` is more than 60 min from the nearest of those.

**SUPPRESSION ORDER (v1.23, 2026-08-14).** This table computes the raw classification. Two suppression steps then run against it, in this order, before any verdict is final: **Step 0.5c** (HOST-UNAVAILABLE — was the slot even fireable? applies to any cadence, including daily) then **Step 0.5b** (HISTORICAL — has this already been reported? weekly+ cadence only). A finding must survive both to reach ⚠️ severity or Step 2's alert email.

## Step 0.5b — Carried-forward suppression (NEW v1.17, 2026-07-30; extended to weekly cadence v1.20, 2026-08-12)

**Purpose:** Step 0.5's table has no decay logic — a monthly/quarterly/annual-cadence task whose most recent due-date fire was late or missed stays classified ALERT-LATE/MISSED every single day until its *next* cadence slot arrives, even though the finding was already reported (once) and nothing new has happened since. The 2026-07-30 run carried 5 such items (a 07-02 monthly slot fulfilled 10 days late, three quarterly/annual tasks whose `lastRunAt` reflected the documented 2026-07-12 mass recreate-to-retag event rather than a real cron fire, and a 07-15 monthly fire that was +178min late) — all already stale news, none of them new that day, none due again for weeks. Left unfixed, this would silently re-trigger the same 5 (plus any new ones) identically every day this monitor runs until each task's next slot.

**Applies to cron tasks whose cadence is weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual** (daily tasks cycle fast enough that this decay problem doesn't practically arise — their next slot is always ≤24h away, so leave their classification as Step 0.5 computes it, no suppression).

**Weekly extension, motivated by the 2026-08-12 run:** the original v1.17 scope excluded weekly tasks on the assumption they "cycle fast enough" not to need this — wrong whenever a multi-day outage lands mid-week. That run carried 3 weekly-cadence items for 1-3 consecutive days each, all re-deriving the same already-explained root cause from scratch every day: `ora-friday-closeout` (real Fri 08-07 slot missed entirely, serviced by a Sun 08-09 catch-up fire — first reported 08-09, still due to re-flag identically through 08-13, the day before its next real Friday slot), `ora-session-sweep` (real Sun 08-09 slot serviced by a Mon 08-10 mount-failure-recovery fire — first reported 08-10, due to re-flag through 08-15), and `ora-ic-memo-pipeline-reconciliation` (Tue 08-11 slot serviced during that day's ~28h outage recovery burst — first reported 08-11, due to re-flag through 08-17). A weekly task's next slot can be up to 7 days out, which is exactly the same decay window a monthly task has relative to a daily run of this monitor — there is no principled reason to suppress one and not the other. The mechanism below is identical for both; only the applicable cadence set changed.

1. Before finalizing a MISSED/ALERT-LATE/OUT-OF-WINDOW verdict for a weekly+ cadence task, search Notion (parent `32b6ecd8-9e59-81f2-b329-df3fd0e25062`) for a prior `Automation Health — YYYY-MM-DD` page that already reported this same `taskId` with a matching `lastRunAt` (or, for the mass-recreate-cluster case, a `lastRunAt` timestamp already explained in a prior report as recreation-artifact rather than a real fire).
2. If a prior report is found AND the task's `nextRunAt`/next cadence slot has not yet arrived (i.e., nothing has changed since that prior report), reclassify as **HISTORICAL** (ℹ️ — one line: verdict + delta + a pointer to the first Notion page that reported it, e.g. `carried forward, first reported YYYY-MM-DD — see [page]`). Do not re-run the full ⚠️ severity or re-trigger Step 2's alert email for it.
3. If no prior report is found (this is a genuinely NEW lateness/staleness finding — the task's most recent cadence slot just passed and this is the first run to see it), classify normally per Step 0.5's table (MISSED/ALERT-LATE/etc., ⚠️) — suppression only applies to items already reported at least once.
4. If the task's cadence slot has advanced since the prior report (e.g. it's now overdue for a *newer* slot than the one already reported), treat as a fresh finding — re-evaluate normally, do not carry forward a stale suppression across cadence boundaries. For a weekly task this means: once its NEXT weekly slot has come and gone, a fresh miss on that new slot is never suppressed by a HISTORICAL pointer to the prior week's incident.
5. **COVERING SEARCH IS THE RATIFIED METHOD (v1.23, 2026-08-14 — replaces "one Notion search per candidate task").** Run **ONE** `notion-search` for `Automation Health` scoped to the Ops Hub parent (`page_url` = `32b6ecd8-9e59-81f2-b329-df3fd0e25062`, `page_size` 10, `max_highlight_length` 0). That single call returns the recent report pages with their dates, which is everything items 1–4 need: a candidate is suppressed when a prior page exists **dated after that task's `lastRunAt`** and its next cadence slot has not yet arrived. Per-task searching is not more rigorous here — it is the same evidence retrieved N times. The 2026-08-14 run carried 15 suppression candidates, which the literal reading would have cost 15 searches; one covering search resolved all 15 and is strictly cheaper *and* strictly stricter, because it also shows you the pages that DON'T exist.

   Two standing constraints on the cheaper method:
   - **State the basis, don't overclaim it.** A covering search establishes *that a dated prior page exists covering the window* — not that you re-read its body. Say so in the report, in those terms. (The 2026-08-14 page's wording is the reference: "a covering Ops Hub search confirmed Automation Health pages exist for … each dated after the corresponding task's `lastRunAt`. Per-page verbatim re-verification was not performed.")
   - **Fetch the page only when the covering search is ambiguous** — i.e. a prior page's date lands within ±1 cadence slot of the `lastRunAt` and you cannot tell from the date alone whether that page could have seen the fire. Then, and only then, spend one `notion-fetch` on that one page.

   On search failure: fall back to Step 0.5's un-suppressed classification and note the fallback in the report rather than guessing.

## Step 0.5c — HOST-UNAVAILABLE suppression (NEW v1.23, 2026-08-14)

**A cron slot that fell while the machine was asleep is not a fleet defect, and reporting it as one is how this monitor generates its own noise.** Step 0.5b decays *repeat* reports of one finding. This step handles the other half: a lateness finding that is NEW today but was never the fleet's fault, because the host was not running when the slot came round.

**The retime option was investigated on 2026-08-14 and REJECTED — do not re-propose it.** The 2026-08-14 report's own workflow-optimization list recommended moving the AM cluster later, on the reasoning that had already worked for the Monday tasks. The evidence does not support it. `maintenance/host-availability-report.py` (added same day; re-run it before revisiting this) takes the first scheduler dispatch of each calendar day from the rotated `main*.log` set — the operative fact, since a slot earlier than first-availability cannot fire on time wherever it is placed. Over the 14 weekdays 2026-07-27 → 2026-08-14:

| First dispatch | Weekdays |
|---|---|
| earliest | **03:31** |
| median | **08:53** |
| latest | **17:09** |
| spread | **818 min (13.6 h)** |

On-time rate by hypothetical slot: 06:00 → 2/14 (14%) · 08:00 → 5/14 (36%) · 09:00 → 8/14 (57%) · 10:00 → 9/14 (64%) · **11:00 → 11/14 (79%)**. There is no slot that reliably works: even 11:00 fails one weekday in five, and moving a *morning* brief to 11:00 defeats its purpose (Block C windows on overnight mail). The Monday retimes succeeded against a *narrow, repeating* Monday-morning pattern; this is an 818-minute spread with no stable shape. **The host's availability is the unpredictable variable — the cron slot is not the lever.**

So the fix belongs here, in classification, not in the schedule:

1. **Precondition.** Step 0.03 has NOT declared MULTI-DAY-OUTAGE (that has its own headline and its own treatment). Establish a same-day host-wake signal from evidence already gathered, in this order — do not re-derive one that exists:
   - `ora-health-guard` Block A.0's wake-lateness alert for today (it computes minutes-late against its own 05:15 ET target on every fire and alerts above 45 min), or
   - the Connector Pre-Flight beacon's `wake_cause` / late-wake note naming today, or
   - failing both, one run of `host-availability-report.py`.
2. Set `hostAvailableAt` = the earliest confirmed scheduler dispatch of the day.
3. **For a task whose `expectedFireTime` < `hostAvailableAt`:** the slot was structurally unfireable. If its `lastRunAt` falls within **60 minutes after** `hostAvailableAt`, the task fired as soon as it could — classify **HOST-UNAVAILABLE** (ℹ️), one line, citing the headline by reference. Do **not** classify MISSED/ALERT-LATE/OUT-OF-WINDOW, and do **not** let it trigger Step 2's alert email.
4. **This step keeps its teeth.** If `lastRunAt` is more than 60 minutes after `hostAvailableAt`, the host WAS up and the task still did not run — that is a real finding and keeps its full Step 0.5 severity. Same if `expectedFireTime` ≥ `hostAvailableAt`: the machine was already awake when the slot came round, so lateness is the fleet's own.
5. Report the HOST-UNAVAILABLE set as a single grouped line naming the incident once (`N tasks — slots fell inside the HH:MM–HH:MM host-unavailable window, all fired within X min of resume`), never as N independent findings. This is the same one-incident discipline Step 0.03 and Step 0.55's Block A.0 row already impose.

**What this would have changed on 2026-08-14:** `ora-cowork-heartbeat`'s 05:50 ET slot fell inside a 00:28–07:57 Modern Standby window and it dispatched at 07:57:23 — the same second the host resumed, 0 minutes after `hostAvailableAt`. It was reported ALERT-LATE ⚠️ and generated an alert email whose entire content was "the thing you were already told about twice." Under this step it is HOST-UNAVAILABLE ℹ️, the run has zero ⚠️ findings, and Step 2 sends nothing.
